Phases of a dynamic liver study

Pre-contrast

The baseline. You need it to know whether a lesion is truly enhancing or already bright, such as T1-bright hemorrhage in an adenoma or a fat-containing nodule. On CT, it shows calcification and how dense the lesion is.

Early arterial, about 15–25 s

Arteries are bright and the portal vein is still dark. This phase is good for an arterial map before surgery, but too early for lesions, because hypervascular tumors have not filled yet.

Late arterial, about 30–40 s

Hepatic arteries are fully enhanced and the portal vein is starting to fill, but the hepatic veins are not yet. Hypervascular lesions (FNH, adenoma, HCC, hypervascular metastases, flash-filling hemangioma) are most conspicuous here. Liver protocols aim for this phase.

Portal venous, about 60–80 s

The portal and hepatic veins are enhanced and the liver is at peak enhancement. This is the best phase to find hypovascular lesions such as colorectal metastases. Hypervascular lesions start to fade, and washout begins to show.

Delayed (equilibrium), about 3 min

Contrast is in equilibrium between blood and interstitium, and the liver starts to fade. Capsule appearance, washout, and delayed enhancement of fibrous tissue (FNH scar, cholangiocarcinoma, fibrosis) are best shown here.

Washout versus delayed enhancement

Both are relative to the liver. Washout means the lesion becomes darker than liver after being bright, because cellular tumor with little interstitium loses contrast. Delayed enhancement means a lesion keeps gaining contrast as it leaks into a large fibrous interstitium. HCC washes out; cholangiocarcinoma and fibrous scars fill in.
