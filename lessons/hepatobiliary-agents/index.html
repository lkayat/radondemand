Hepatobiliary contrast agents

Gadoxetate (Eovist, Primovist) and gadobenate (MultiHance) next to a standard extracellular agent, on the same liver and the same lesions. Scrub from the arterial phase to two hours, watch hepatocytes take up the agent and excrete it into the bile, and see why FNH lights up, adenomas and metastases go dark, hemangiomas "wash out" when they do not, and an early HCC can show itself before it turns hypervascular.

How the hepatobiliary phase works

Gadoxetate enters hepatocytes through OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 on the sinusoidal side and leaves into bile through MRP2; a little returns to the blood through MRP3. What a lesion looks like in the hepatobiliary phase depends on how much it takes in and how well it drains. Pick a tissue to see its transporters.
