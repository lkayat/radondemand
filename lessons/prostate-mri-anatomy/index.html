Zones and signal

Peripheral zone

Posterior and lateral, wrapping around to the apex; about 70% of the glandular tissue in a young man. Bright on T2 because of its loose glandular stroma, high on ADC. The anterior horns reach forward around the transition zone. Most cancers (about 70%) arise here.

Transition zone

Two small lobes beside the proximal urethra, about 5% of the young gland, and the site of BPH. Heterogeneous: stromal nodules are dark on T2 and lower on ADC, glandular nodules bright, both usually encapsulated. A dark surgical pseudocapsule separates it from the peripheral zone.

Central zone

A cone at the posterior base around the ejaculatory ducts, apex at the verumontanum. Dense stroma makes it symmetric and dark on T2, lower on ADC, without early enhancement: a classic mimic of cancer. Fewer cancers start here, but they tend to be aggressive.

Anterior fibromuscular stroma

Non-glandular smooth muscle and fibrous tissue on the anterior surface, thickest toward the apex. Dark on T2 and ADC with low, late enhancement. Symmetric and crescentic; asymmetric thickening or mass effect suggests an anterior tumor.

Capsule and surroundings

The capsule is a thin fibromuscular band, not a true capsule, absent at the apex and anteriorly. Neurovascular bundles run posterolaterally at about 5 and 7 o'clock. Periprostatic veins (Santorini plexus anteriorly) are bright on T2 and enhance. Denonvilliers fascia separates the gland from the rectum.

Seminal vesicles and ducts

Seminal vesicles are coiled fluid-filled sacs, bright on T2 with thin dark walls, above the base and behind the bladder. Each joins its vas deferens (ampulla) to form an ejaculatory duct that runs through the central zone to the verumontanum.

Straight versus oblique axial

Why the plane matters

The long axis of the prostate is tilted relative to the scanner, running from the base (posterosuperior, at the bladder neck) to the apex (anteroinferior). A straight axial slice therefore crosses the apex anteriorly and the base posteriorly at the same time, so zones and PI-RADS levels mix within one image.

Oblique planes

Prescribing the axial plane perpendicular to the gland's long axis on the sagittal image (and the coronal plane parallel to it) gives uniform sampling: each axial slice belongs to one level, the central zone appears as a symmetric band at the base, and measurements and sector assignments are consistent between studies. Keep DWI and DCE in the same plane as T2 for side-by-side reading.

Try it

Set the axial plane to straight and watch the Apex / Mid / Base bands appear across a single axial image, while the yellow line on the sagittal view shows the plane cutting obliquely through the gland. Then press "Oblique axial (⟂ gland)". Change the prostate tilt to see how the effect grows with the angle.

Measurements

Volume (PI-RADS v2.1)

Ellipsoid formula: AP × TR × CC × 0.52. Measure AP and CC on the midsagittal T2 image (maximum dimensions) and TR at the widest axial level. PSA density = PSA / volume; values ≥ 0.15 ng/mL² are commonly used as a threshold for biopsy decisions, though cut-offs vary.

Ellipsoid versus true volume

The ellipsoid formula assumes a smooth gland. Large, lobulated BPH, a median lobe or a post-TURP/HoLEP cavity make it less accurate; the table compares your ellipsoid estimate with the model's true tissue volume.

Membranous urethral length

From the prostatic apex to the entry of the urethra into the penile bulb on the midsagittal image. A longer membranous urethra is associated with better continence after radical prostatectomy.

BPH and the vanishing peripheral zone

As the transition zone enlarges it pushes the peripheral zone posterolaterally and compresses it against the capsule. In marked BPH the peripheral zone is a thin rim, and at the extreme it is effaced to the pseudocapsule. Cancers then arise in a peripheral zone you can barely see, and transition-zone nodules can bulge into territory that used to be peripheral zone. Move the BPH slider and watch the posterior rim.

After treatment

TURP

A funnel-shaped defect of the proximal (supramontanal) urethra, widest at the bladder neck, filled with urine and continuous with the bladder. Residual transition zone remains laterally; the peripheral zone is preserved.

HoLEP

The adenoma is enucleated to the surgical capsule, leaving a large fluid-filled fossa surrounded by a thin rim of compressed peripheral zone. The gland volume falls dramatically; the ellipsoid formula then overestimates the tissue.

UroLift

Implants hold the lateral lobes apart. The nitinol capsular tabs cause little artifact; the stainless-steel urethral end pieces produce blooming in the posterior transition zone, about 8 mm on T2, 10 mm on DCE and nearly 3 cm on DWI in one series, which can hide adjacent tissue.

External-beam radiation

The gland shrinks and becomes diffusely low on T2 with loss of zonal differentiation; ADC falls and enhancement decreases. Seminal vesicles atrophy and the rectal wall may thicken. Recurrence is sought as focal early enhancement and restricted diffusion, often near the original tumor site.

Gold fiducials

Small seeds placed for radiation targeting: tiny signal voids on T2 that bloom on gradient-echo and much more on EPI diffusion. Do not mistake them for calcifications or call the blooming tissue abnormal.

Hemorrhage and prostatitis

Post-biopsy hemorrhage is low on T2 and bright on T1; tumor tends to spare it (hemorrhage exclusion sign). Prostatitis gives band-like or wedge-shaped, mildly low T2 signal with mild ADC reduction, without a discrete mass.

Where cancers arise

About 70% of cancers arise in the peripheral zone, 20–25% in the transition zone, and about 5% in the central zone (often more aggressive). PI-RADS v2.1 uses T2 as the dominant sequence for the transition zone and DWI for the peripheral zone; the lesion is assigned to the zone it arises from, so knowing the anatomy above is the first step in scoring. Lesion scoring itself is a separate lesson.
