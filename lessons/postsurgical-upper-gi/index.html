    Choosing the contrast

    Early after surgery, the study usually starts with water-soluble iodinated contrast, because barium that leaks out of the bowel stays there. Water-soluble contrast is safer if it escapes, but it is less dense, does not coat the mucosa, and misses small leaks. Most protocols therefore run water-soluble contrast first and, if no leak is seen and concern remains, finish with thin barium. Later studies, done to look for strictures, ulcers or function, use barium from the start. Follow your department's protocol; the evidence behind this approach is summarized below.

    Why water-soluble first

    Barium outside the bowel is not absorbed. In the mediastinum or peritoneum it can cause mediastinitis or peritonitis, granulomas and adhesions, and it lingers on later CT. Water-soluble iodinated contrast is absorbed if it leaks.

    Federle et al, Abdom Radiol 2017

    Why water-soluble is not enough

    Iodinated contrast is less dense and does not coat. In 18 patients with a negative water-soluble study for suspected esophageal perforation, barium then showed a perforation in 4 (22%), with no complications from the barium.

    A surgical series used dilute barium alone for postoperative esophageal leaks and saw no mediastinitis over months of follow-up.

    Buecker et al, Radiology 1997 · Gollub and Bains, Radiology 1997

    Which iodinated agent

    Diatrizoate (Gastrografin) is hyperosmolar and ionic. Aspirated into the lungs, it can cause pulmonary edema. When aspiration is likely, as after esophagectomy, use a low-osmolar nonionic agent such as iohexol, and give a test sip first.

    Hyperosmolar contrast also draws water into the bowel, so it fades as it moves distally. Try diatrizoate in the viewer and watch the small bowel.

    Federle et al 2017 · Radiopaedia, fluoroscopic evaluation of oesophagectomy

    Know the limits

    A swallow study can miss a leak that matters. After esophagectomy, barium detected 13 of 36 clinically significant leaks (sensitivity 36%, specificity 97%). In 277 sleeve gastrectomies, the routine upper GI series detected none of the leaks or strictures; the one leak was found on CT after discharge. Clinical signs, drains and CT carry equal weight.

    Roh et al, Korean J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2016 · Rossi and Lutfi, SAGES abstract
