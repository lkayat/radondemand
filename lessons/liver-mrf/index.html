Liver MR Fingerprinting: separating fat, iron, inflammation and fibrosis

Diffuse liver disease rarely comes one process at a time. Fat, iron, inflammation and fibrosis all change the liver's relaxation times, often in opposite directions, so a single T1 value can look normal in a sick liver. This page simulates a breath-hold liver MRF acquisition with a multi-echo readout, separates water from fat, estimates R2*, and matches the water-only fingerprint to a dictionary. You can then see how each process moves each measurement.

2The T1 tug-of-war

Inflammation and fibrosis expand the extracellular water space and lengthen T1. Iron shortens it. Fat, if not separated, drags the apparent T1 down further because fat's T1 is short. A normal-looking T1 can therefore hide real disease. This is why liver T1 is only interpretable alongside PDFF and R2*, and why iron-corrected T1 exists.

3How multi-echo MRF pulls water, fat and iron apart

Each TR reads several echoes. Between echoes, fat precesses away from water (chemical shift) while everything decays with R2*. Fitting the echoes separates a water fingerprint from a fat fingerprint and returns R2*. Only the water fingerprint is matched to the dictionary, so T1 and T2 describe liver water itself. It is done here in the SVD domain, as practical reconstructions do.

4What the evidence shows

Histology correlation

AUC 0.98 / 0.92 / 0.74 / 0.62

For steatosis, inflammation, siderosis and fibrosis (moderate–severe versus none–mild) in 23 biopsied patients, using water–fat-separated liver MRF at 1.5 T in a single roughly 18 s breath hold. PDFF tracked steatosis very strongly, T1 rose with inflammation grade, and fibrosis was the weakest signal.

Fujita et al., Radiology 2023

Repeatability in patients

CV 3.2 / 5.5 / 7.1 / 4.6%

Scan–rescan coefficients of variation for liver T1, T2, T2* and PDFF in 56 participants with diffuse liver disease. MRF T1 ran about 99 ms higher than MOLLI and T2 about 11 ms lower than GraSE, so thresholds do not transfer between methods.

Fujita et al., Radiology 2023

Cleveland liver MRF

A 2D FISP-based liver MRF at 3 T maps T1 and T2 in a 15 s breath hold per slice, with repeatability and reproducibility evaluated in volunteers and motion examined as a confounder. The earlier abdominal MRF work added B1 mapping to correct flip-angle errors within a single breath hold.

Lu, Stephans, Qiu, Tirumani, Chen, Med Phys 2025; Chen et al., Radiology 2016

Beyond T1 and T2

Newer schedules add T2*, off-resonance, T1ρ (proposed for fibrosis sensitivity) or diffusion, and free-breathing acquisitions with pilot-tone navigation have been tested across scanners. Fibrosis staging still leans on MR elastography.

Kretzler et al., MAGMA 2025; Velasco et al., MRM 2022

Always read T1 with PDFF and R2*. T1 alone cannot distinguish a healthy liver from an inflamed, iron-loaded one. Check whether the T1 you are given is water-specific or a mixed water–fat value.

Use method-specific reference ranges. MRF, MOLLI, variable flip angle and cT1 products give systematically different numbers for the same liver, and field strength matters too.

Check R2* before trusting T1 and T2. At high R2* the signal is gone after the first echoes, and T2 and PDFF become unreliable. This happens sooner at 3 T.

Mind B1 at 3 T. Large, smooth gradients across the liver in T1 or T2 suggest uncorrected transmit-field variation. Confirm whether B1 was mapped or the schedule is B1-insensitive.

Place large ROIs away from vessels, bile ducts and the dome. Sample several segments. Heterogeneous fibrosis and focal fat sparing are common, and the dome is prone to breathing and susceptibility artifacts.

Keep fibrosis in perspective. Relaxometry is sensitive to inflammation and edema, which can rise and fall. Stiffness from MR elastography remains the imaging reference for fibrosis stage.

Teaching simulation. The acquisition is a FISP-MRF schedule with a three-echo readout, standing in for the several published liver MRF designs (preparation-block, quadratic-RF and multi-echo variants). Tissue values and every disease effect on T1, T2 and R2* are illustrative and chosen to show directions and interactions, not calibrated to histology or to any product. The iron-corrected T1 here uses this simulator's own iron model; commercial cT1 uses a different, proprietary calibration. Signals are real-valued with complex echo phases from a six-peak fat model; there is no motion, off-resonance, slice-profile or magnetization-transfer modeling. For teaching only; not for clinical use.
