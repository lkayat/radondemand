HCC: from fibrosis to LI-RADS and treatment

The natural history of hepatocellular carcinoma has a handful of checkpoints where imaging makes the decision: is there fibrosis, is this patient at risk, is that nodule HCC, how far has it spread, and did the treatment work. This lesson follows a liver along that path, from chronic injury to stepwise carcinogenesis, LI-RADS categorization, staging and treatment response.

1. Chronic liver disease and fibrosis

Pick a cause and move the fibrosis stage. Morphologic signs of cirrhosis appear late and are insensitive; MR elastography measures stiffness directly and tracks fibrosis long before the contour changes.

LI-RADS applicability

MR elastography

Draw ROIs in the right lobe on the confidence-masked elastogram, avoiding the liver edge, fissures, large vessels and lesions; report the mean. Thresholds vary slightly by vendor and study; congestion, inflammation, cholestasis and a recent meal raise stiffness too.

2. Stepwise carcinogenesis

In a cirrhotic liver, HCC usually develops in steps: regenerative nodule, low- and high-grade dysplastic nodule, early HCC, a focus of HCC inside the nodule, then progressed HCC. Along the way the nodule loses its portal tracts, gains unpaired arteries, and loses hepatocyte transporters (OATP1B3). Pick a step in the diagram, the buttons or the image matrix.

Algorithm, step by step

    Washout with gadoxetate

    Judge washout on the portal venous phase only. Transitional and hepatobiliary hypointensity are ancillary features, not washout (Lesson 2).

    Arterioportal shunts

    Wedge-shaped, peripheral, no mass effect, vessels course through, invisible on other phases: LR-1 or LR-2, not LR-3.

    Confluent fibrosis

    Wedge-shaped, capsular retraction, delayed enhancement, often anterior right or medial segments. Delayed enhancement is not a capsule and not washout.

    Hemangioma in cirrhosis

    Less common and often sclerosed. Use T2 and blood-pool matching; with gadoxetate expect pseudo-washout.

    LR-M is not HCC-specific

    Targetoid observations include iCCA, combined HCC-CCA and atypical HCC. Biopsy is usually needed before treatment or transplant listing.

    10–19 mm, nonrim APHE

    Capsule alone gives LR-4; washout or threshold growth alone gives LR-5. Two or more additional features always give LR-5.

    4. Treatment response (LI-RADS v2024)

    Since 2024 there are two CT/MRI algorithms: one after nonradiation therapies (ablation, TACE, bland embolization) and one after radiation-based therapies (TARE/Y-90, SBRT). Viability is now any masslike enhancement in any phase; washout is no longer required. Radiation-treated tumors keep enhancing for months, hence the "nonprogressing" category. Restricted diffusion and mild–moderate T2 hyperintensity at the same site are optional ancillary features, used at the radiologist's discretion: the base category follows the enhancement, and the lesson shows the category you would reach if you choose to apply the upgrade. After radiation, only features that are new or increased over time can be used.

    5. Ultrasound surveillance (LI-RADS US v2024)

    Surveillance ultrasound every 6 months, with or without AFP, for patients at risk. Each exam gets a category for the most suspicious finding and a visualization score that says how much of the liver could really be seen.

    6. Checkpoints: size, multiplicity, interval change, vascular invasion

    Size

    Small HCC (under 2 cm) comes in two flavors: early HCC (vaguely nodular, often without arterial hyperenhancement) and small progressed HCC (hypervascular). Large HCC (over 5 cm) is more often poorly differentiated, with microvascular invasion, satellites and metastases.

    Multifocality

    Multicentric HCC: separate primaries, often in different segments and at different steps of carcinogenesis. Intrahepatic metastases: satellites around a dominant tumor, usually in the same segment or downstream along the portal flow.

    Interval change

    Threshold growth (≥50% in ≤6 months) is a major feature. Subthreshold growth is an ancillary feature. Rapid growth, ill-defined margins and vascular involvement on follow-up mark progression.

    Microvascular invasion (predictors)

    Not visible directly, but suggested by a disrupted capsule, non-smooth margin, irregular circumferential peritumoral arterial enhancement, peritumoral hypointensity in the hepatobiliary phase, arterial rim enhancement, large size and multifocality.

    Bland thrombus or tumor in vein?

    Tumor in vein enhances in the arterial phase, washes out like the parent tumor, expands the vein, restricts diffusion and is usually contiguous with the mass. Bland thrombus does not enhance (check subtraction if it is T1 bright) and does not expand the vessel.

    7. Staging and treatment: BCLC 2022

    Resection

    Best for a single tumor in a patient without clinically significant portal hypertension and with normal bilirubin; limited by liver reserve in most cirrhotic patients.

    Ablation

    Radiofrequency or microwave for small tumors (ideally ≤2–3 cm); first choice for BCLC 0 and for small BCLC A tumors when resection or transplant is not chosen.

    Transplantation

    Treats tumor and cirrhosis together. Milan criteria: one tumor ≤5 cm, or up to three tumors each ≤3 cm, with no vascular invasion or extrahepatic spread. Locoregional therapy can bridge or downstage.

    TACE and TARE

    TACE for intermediate-stage multinodular disease with preserved portal flow. Radioembolization (Y-90) is an alternative, including for bridging, downstaging and some tumors with segmental portal vein invasion.

    SBRT

    Stereotactic radiation for tumors unsuitable for ablation (location, size). Assess with the radiation treatment-response algorithm.

    Systemic therapy

    First line for advanced HCC is now immunotherapy-based (atezolizumab–bevacizumab or durvalumab–tremelimumab); sorafenib, lenvatinib and durvalumab alone are alternatives.
