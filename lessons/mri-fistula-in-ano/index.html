Classification

Anatomy that decides the classification

Two sphincters, one plane between

The internal sphincter is the thickened circular smooth muscle of the bowel wall, intermediate signal on T2. The external sphincter is striated, dark on T2, and continues upward into the puborectalis and levator ani. The intersphincteric space between them is a thin fat plane: the highway for intersphincteric tracts and abscesses.

Where fistulas start

Most fistulas are cryptoglandular: an anal gland at the dentate line (mid anal canal) becomes infected, and the infection tracks outward through the internal sphincter into the intersphincteric space. Locate the internal opening by its clock position and its level relative to the dentate line.

Clock face

This lesson uses the anatomical clock on axial images: 12 o'clock anterior, 6 o'clock posterior, 3 o'clock patient left, 9 o'clock patient right. Surgeons may use the lithotomy position, where the same picture is described differently; state the convention in the report to avoid wrong-side surgery.

Plane planning

Coronal-oblique images are angled parallel to the anal canal and axial-oblique images perpendicular to it. Off-axis axial images cut the sphincter ring obliquely, so a tract can appear to cross at the wrong level. Try the plane-tilt slider.

Structured report

Protocol

Sequences

Small field of view, high-resolution T2-weighted imaging without fat suppression in sagittal, coronal-oblique and axial-oblique planes for anatomy; fat-suppressed T2 or STIR for tract and abscess conspicuity; fat-suppressed T1-weighted images after contrast for activity and to separate abscess from inflamed tissue; DWI as an adjunct. Plan oblique planes on the sagittal images along the anal canal.

What the surgeon needs

Internal opening (clock and level), primary tract course and its relation to the sphincters and levator, how much external sphincter would be divided by a fistulotomy, secondary tracts and horseshoe spread, abscesses that need drainage, supralevator extension, and in Crohn's disease activity and proctitis.

Reading signal

Active tracts contain fluid or pus (bright on fat-suppressed T2, high on DWI) with enhancing granulation walls. Healing tracts become thin and dark on T2 with little enhancement. An abscess is a collection with a rim and no central enhancement; an inflamed tract without a collection enhances throughout.
